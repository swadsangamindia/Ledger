Digital bookkeeping platforms like Khata Bahi eliminate balance discrepancies by automating running balances and creating verifiable audit trails for both buyers and sellers.

1 Maintaining a Daily Single-Entry Cash Register (Khata)

In single-entry bookkeeping, transactions are logged either as incoming cash (Receipt / Jama) or outgoing cash (Payment / Udhar). Unlike enterprise double-entry accounting with complex debits and credits, a single-entry cash ledger prioritizes immediate cash position visibility for Kirana shops and retail counters.

  • Opening Cash Balance: Tally the exact cash physically present in the cash drawer each morning.
  • Real-Time Entry: Log payments the moment cash or UPI transfers settle to avoid memory drift.
  • Daily Reconciliation: Compute Opening Balance + Receipts - Payments. If drawer cash deviates from this total, audit leakage immediately.

2 Best Practices for Customer Credit (Udhar) Recovery

Customer credit is essential in neighbourhood trade, but uncontrolled credit chokes store liquidity:

  • Credit Ceiling: Establish a strict cap per customer (e.g., ₹2,000 or 15 days). Stop extending further goods until partial repayment occurs.
  • Weekly Statements: Send WhatsApp or SMS balance summaries every Sunday rather than waiting for month-end bill confrontations.
  • Early Settlement Incentives: Offer 1–2% discounts on bills settled within 48 hours to accelerate inventory turnover.

3 Understanding GST Slabs: CGST, SGST, and Invoicing Rules

The Goods and Services Tax (GST) requires transparent breakdown on taxable commercial invoices:

  • Intrastate Sales: Total GST is split evenly between Central (CGST) and State (SGST). For example, 18% GST = 9% CGST + 9% SGST.
  • Tax Invoice vs. Payment Voucher: Invoices represent commercial supply with tax liabilities; Payment vouchers confirm monetary fund receipt without generating additional tax.
